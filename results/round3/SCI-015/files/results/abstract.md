# Optimized Abstract

We present a synthetic information-theoretic framework for studying neural correlates of consciousness by integrating approximate integrated information, perturbational complexity, and global workspace broadcasting. The framework combines four reusable Python modules for IIT-style state-transition analysis, PCI-oriented TMS-EEG simulation, global workspace ignition dynamics, and general information metrics including entropy, mutual information, transfer entropy, and Lempel-Ziv complexity. Across five experiments, Gaussian integrated information declined with increasing anesthesia depth (slope = -0.025 ± 0.013, r = -0.658, p = 0.0012), while simulated PCI distributions separated vegetative state, minimally conscious state, and control cohorts with large effect sizes. A disorder-of-consciousness classifier using Phi, PCI, global broadcast strength, and spectral entropy achieved realistic cross-validated macro-AUC values of 0.900–0.918 and accuracies of 0.711–0.767, avoiding implausible perfect discrimination. Global workspace broadcast efficiency showed a strong positive association with normalized global Phi (r = 0.993, 95% CI [0.981, 0.998]), supporting partial convergence between IIT-style and GWT-style markers in the simulation environment. Transfer entropy was higher in awake than anesthetized networks, consistent with richer information flow under preserved consciousness. These results suggest that a composite NCC representation can capture complementary aspects of integration, perturbational complexity, and broadcast dynamics, while also highlighting that external validation on real TMS-EEG and clinical EEG datasets remains essential.
