# Literature Review

Recent connectome studies support a multimodal pipeline that combines structural tractography, resting-state functional connectivity, graph-theoretic summaries, and predictive modeling, but they also show that biomarker performance is constrained by reliability and methodological sensitivity. In schizophrenia, multilayer dynamic community analysis demonstrated increased flexibility in subcortical, cerebellar, and frontoparietal systems, suggesting that dynamic modular organization may provide disease-sensitive information beyond static correlation matrices, although the effect was not always superior to simpler metrics (Gifford, 2020). A more translational direction is visible in recent connectome-based predictive modeling work, where resting-state connectivity patterns predicted antipsychotic response with moderate generalization in an independent cohort (Liu, 2025). Together with the broader review by Abi-Dargham et al. (2023), these studies support using moderately complex but interpretable connectivity biomarkers instead of relying on any single edge-level effect.

For Alzheimer-related structural connectivity, recent work emphasizes the value of tractography-derived biomarkers rather than local diffusion measures alone. Solders et al. (2022) showed that specialized diffusion tractography can reconstruct biologically meaningful pathways implicated in tau spread, while Wang et al. (2024) used edge-density imaging to identify white-matter patterns linked to both cognition and conversion-relevant phenotypes. Ren et al. (2020) further demonstrated that structural-functional mismatch may be clinically informative, with structural abnormalities concentrating in subcortical and default-mode regions whereas functional changes were more heterogeneous across patients. Taghvaei et al. (2024) added evidence that disconnection mediated cognitive deficits in ADNI participants, reinforcing the notion that clinically relevant biomarkers may emerge from network disconnection patterns rather than isolated lesions.

A recurring theme across the literature is that reliability is a major bottleneck. Camp et al. (2024) found poor average edge-wise ICC despite stronger multivariate reliability, and Knodt et al. (2023) reported that principal gradient measures are more reliable than conventional edge-wise functional connectivity. These findings justify including both ICC and overlap-based structural reproducibility metrics in a connectome pipeline. They also motivate the use of summary graph metrics and machine-learning features derived from many connections instead of over-interpreting single-edge results.

Methodologically, several gaps remain. First, dynamic FC measures can be informative but are sensitive to windowing choices and preprocessing. Second, structural tractography outcomes vary with probabilistic versus deterministic assumptions and with thresholding rules. Third, many disease-classification studies report promising discrimination but are limited by modest sample size, site effects, and incomplete external validation. Therefore, the present simulation pipeline adopts realistic but conservative classification targets, integrates graph summaries with connectivity matrices, and explicitly reports uncertainty and reliability alongside classification performance.

## Key design implications for this project
- Multimodal integration is justified because structural and functional abnormalities are partly overlapping but not redundant.
- Dynamic FC should be included, but results should be interpreted cautiously due to parameter sensitivity.
- Graph-theoretic measures such as small-worldness, modularity, and hub centrality remain useful as compact network descriptors.
- Disease biomarker evaluation should target realistic AUC values rather than perfect separation.
- Reliability assessment is mandatory because recent studies show edge-wise FC is often only modestly reproducible.
