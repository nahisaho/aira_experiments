Urban heat island intensification in central Tokyo is projected to worsen substantially by 2050 because climate warming interacts with dense canyon morphology and increasing cooling demand. We developed a reduced-form but physically informed simulation framework that integrates an urban canopy model, anthropogenic heat emissions, mitigation strategies, and WBGT heat-risk diagnostics. The framework reproduced realistic district contrasts in 2024, with mean UHI intensity of 3.53 °C in Shinjuku, 3.01 °C in Shibuya, and 1.86 °C in a suburban reference district. By 2050, Shinjuku increased to 4.41 °C in RCP4.5 and 5.29 °C in RCP8.5. Under RCP8.5, peak WBGT reached 40.18 °C in the baseline case but declined to 38.22 °C under a combined greening and cool-materials strategy. Across five sensitivity runs, baseline peak WBGT was 40.30 ± 0.64 °C (95% CI 39.51–41.10 °C) and the combined scenario was 38.63 ± 0.65 °C (95% CI 37.83–39.44 °C). These results indicate that integrated mitigation is more effective than single-strategy deployment and that WBGT-based danger-day reduction is a useful adaptation endpoint for Tokyo heat planning.
