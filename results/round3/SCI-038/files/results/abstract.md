# Abstract

Active Debris Removal (ADR) mission design is inherently multidisciplinary because a practical mission must decide which objects to remove, in what order to visit them, how to execute the transfers, how to approach each target, and how to capture non-cooperative debris with uncertain rotational states. This paper presents a lightweight but reproducible Python framework that integrates debris scoring, mission-sequence optimization, low-thrust transfer propagation, Hill-frame rendezvous, tumbling-debris simulation, capture-mechanism screening, and consolidated reporting. In the nominal run, total mission delta-v decreases from 12.963 km/s for nearest-neighbor to 12.848 km/s for GA+2opt, but a six-seed sensitivity analysis shows that the average paired reduction is only 0.028 km/s with 95% CI [-0.438, 0.493] and p = 0.885, indicating that the optimizer advantage is not statistically robust in this simplified synthetic setting. A Q-law-inspired transfer from 400 km, 51.6 deg to approximately 800 km, 71 deg yields Δv 0.318 km/s, time 441.9 h, and fuel use 10.76 kg. Hill-frame rendezvous closes an initial range of 5001.2 m with total impulse 1262.1 mm/s. Tumbling analysis estimates a dominant period of 300.1 s, and the harpoon concept exceeds the robotic-arm concept by 0.169 ± 0.157 in modeled capture success probability, with corrected p = 0.092. The main contribution is therefore methodological: an integrated and extensible baseline for ADR mission trade studies rather than a flight-certified optimizer.
